This procedure has been revolutionary to
orthopaedics giving many patients the ability to
return to function, with implants lasting about 20-

Partial knee replacement is ideal for patients
with pain on one side of the knee, strong
ligaments, minimal deformity, and good knee
movement. Compared to total knee
replacement, recovery is usually quicker and
easier, with less blood loss and lower risk of
blood clots. If arthritis progresses to other parts
of the knee it can later be successfully
converted into a total knee replacement.

YOUR SURGERY AND HOSPITAL STAY:
Admission 1 day prior to surgery.
You will be kept nil by mouth since midnight and necessary preparations
will be done.
On day 2 you will be posted for surgery.
Single TKR usually  takes  one hour but the actual elapsed time may vary.
Generally surgery is done under spinal anaesthesia.
- Patient will be shifted to ICU with Foley’s
catheter after surgery and post-op X-ray.  
- ICU observation for 12–16 hours to monitor
vital signs.  
- IV antibiotics, analgesics, antacids,
antiemetics, and epidural anaesthesia will be
administered.  
- If required, a femoral block may be given
by the anaesthetist for better pain relief.
Nill by mouth for 4–6 hours post-surgery.
Gradual shift from liquids to normal diet by Day 2.
Physiotherapy and mobilisation twice daily from
Day 2.
Catheter removal after mobilisation.
Dressing and discharge on Day 3 with
medications and crepe bandage.
Round-the-clock monitoring of vitals, pain, and
intake/output.
Daily review by Dr. Vishal Kasliwal.
